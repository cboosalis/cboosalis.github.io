Learner packet: two reference cards and a simulated dataset

Teacher-written science reference card (original wording; not a research report): Green plants use light energy during photosynthesis. Carbon dioxide and water provide matter used to make sugars; oxygen is released. A simplified overall model is 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2, with light energy entering the process. Count carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms on both sides. Atoms are rearranged, not created from sunlight. Sugar is matter that stores chemical energy. A matter arrow and an energy arrow therefore mean different things. This summary omits the many biochemical steps; it is a model for reasoning about inputs, outputs and energy transformation, not an animation of every reaction.

CDE: HS-LS1-5, modeling photosynthesis

Teacher-written companion reference card (original wording): Plants also carry out cellular respiration. A simplified overall model uses sugar and oxygen and produces carbon dioxide and water. Chemical bonds are rearranged, with a net transfer of energy that can support cellular processes and warm the surroundings. Breaking a chemical bond requires energy; the overall reaction can transfer energy because new bonds form as well. Do not explain respiration as energy being created or as bond breaking alone releasing energy. Photosynthesis needs light; respiration can occur in light and darkness. An oxygen reading from a plant system can show the net result of more than one process, not a direct measurement of photosynthesis by itself.

Keep intellectual responsibility shared—and individual

Collaboration is an intellectual routine, not simply seating students together. Each student first makes a private attempt. In pairs, the explainer states a claim and points to evidence; the listener paraphrases it before asking an evidence question. The original speaker confirms or corrects the paraphrase. Partners switch roles, negotiate a revision, and record what changed and why. Everyone then completes a new individual response. Do not assign the same student permanently to note-taking or translating; both students need opportunities to read, speak, listen, reason and write.

Independent summative task—student copy

Student task: A teacher-created follow-on simulation starts all chambers at 8.0 mg/L dissolved oxygen. After 20 minutes, the plant/light chamber is 8.5, the plant/dark chamber is 7.7, and both no-plant chambers remain 8.0. These are invented instructional numbers, not a second real experiment. Draw a connected model of photosynthesis and respiration; show matter inputs/outputs and energy transfers. Write an explanation that (1) calculates the two plant changes, (2) explains why a positive light result does not show respiration has stopped, (3) uses the no-plant comparison appropriately, and (4) states one limitation. Then independently respond to “Plants only respire at night.”

Conditional re-teach: ready-to-use student check

Use only if actual focus-student evidence identifies a net-change misconception. This is an original conceptual simulation, not laboratory data. In one interval, a lighted plant model shows 7 arbitrary oxygen units produced by photosynthesis and 4 used by respiration. (1) Show both process arrows and calculate the net change. (2) Explain why a positive change does not mean respiration stopped. (3) A different interval has a net change of −2 units. Give two possible production/consumption pairs with that net change, and explain why net change alone does not identify both process amounts. Use a causal connection and one limitation in your explanation.

Before the new check, a teacher may model a different pair of amounts, ask the learner to point to the two processes and hear a paraphrase. Complete the 7/4 and −2 tasks independently afterward, with required accommodations. Explain which support, if any, helped you distinguish a process from a net measurement.

Conditional extension: ready-to-use student inquiry

Use only if actual content and ELD evidence supports extension. Evaluate two proposed investigations; neither is an experiment reported as completed. Plan A compares one plant chamber in light at 22°C with one in darkness at 15°C. Plan B compares repeated matched plant chambers at the same temperature, water volume and biomass, varying light, with matched no-plant comparisons and a checked oxygen sensor. Which design better tests a light-related difference, and why? Identify one remaining inference limit, propose an additional measurement or control, and explain how it could test a claim. Paraphrase a partner’s design critique before writing your independent recommendation.
