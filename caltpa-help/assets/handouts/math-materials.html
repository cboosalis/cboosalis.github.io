Grade 3 · Mathematics · Student problem card

What does each contribution mean?

Student code: __________   Date: __________

Original fundraiser problem

PROBLEM: Adelante school had a fundraiser for two families who lost their homes in a fire. Each student in Maestra C.'s class donated $3.00. There were 27 students in the class. How much money will they be able to give to each family?

First, think independently

  1. Explain who gives money and how much each giver contributes. Point to the words that support your interpretation.
  2. Draw or build a representation. Label what one part represents and what all the parts represent.
  3. Record a way to find the total contribution. Explain how your work matches the situation.

Then discuss and compare

Keep your first work. After discussion, use a different mark to show any change and explain why you made it. Do not erase the first interpretation.

Consider the two families

What assumption do you need about sharing the money? Show and explain how the money could be shared under that assumption.

The story has 27 students; this does not describe the size of the actual teaching group. Teacher: attend sensitively to the real-fire context. Do not supply the multiplication connection before students compare their reasoning.

Reciprocal partner card

Speaker

Explain your interpretation, show your model, and justify your operation with the quantities.

Optional start: “Each ___ contributes ___. My model shows ___ because ___.”

Listener

Restate your partner’s idea before adding yours. Ask a question about the text or model.

Optional start: “I heard you say ___. Where does your model show ___?”

Switch roles. Decide whether you agree using evidence—not who speaks first or most confidently. A different interpretation is an opportunity to investigate.

Whole-group comparison and self-check

How are repeated addition and multiplication related in this problem? What does each number in your expression represent? Why do the methods describe the same total?

What did you check, question, or change? What evidence helped?

Teacher: compare student representations before explicitly consolidating multiplication as repeated addition of equal groups. Provide play money or drawn money for the separate equal-sharing extension; use school-approved materials.

  1. Draw a labeled model. Show what one group represents.
  2. Write a repeated-addition expression and a multiplication expression.
  3. Explain why both expressions match the same situation.
  4. What did you check to make sure your model matches “each”?
