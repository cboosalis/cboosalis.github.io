Section 1 of 3 · Grade 3 · Texts and independent comprehension

Beavers: what does the evidence say?

Student code: __________   Date: __________

Text A · Changes to a place

Beavers live on land and in water. They use branches and other materials to build dams across some streams. A dam holds back water and can form a pond. The pond and nearby wetland can provide habitat for other animals. Beavers also use wet areas to store plant food for winter.

Changes that help wildlife can create problems for people. At Serpentine Hot Springs in Alaska, beaver activity changed the flow of water. The water flooded an airstrip used by aircraft.

Workers installed a device with pipes to move water through the dam. A filter spread the flow and reduced signals that could lead beavers to rebuild it. Small fish could still pass through. Staff monitor the wetland and the device to protect the airstrip while considering the habitat.

Text B · An author’s view

Beavers are the most fascinating builders in nature. Their ponds can provide habitat for other animals. Every class should learn about them.

Text C · A different place

At Zion National Park, beavers often make homes in riverbanks. Frequent floods make dams difficult to maintain. This differs from a place where a dam can remain across a stream.

1. Main idea and supporting evidence

State a main idea for Text A. Explain how two details support it. Write independently before discussing with a partner.

Original instructional adaptations, not quotations or validated leveled texts. Factual basis: National Park Service, Bering Land Bridge: Beaver and Zion: American Beaver. Text B is a deliberately constructed evaluative voice. Teacher: record read-aloud or other access support separately from independent print reading.

3. Author and reader viewpoints

How does Text B’s author view beavers? Which words show this? Explain how your own viewpoint agrees or differs, and why.

4. Individual word reading, meaning, and spelling

Read aloud: rebuild · builder · helpful · repack. Explain the meaningful parts and the whole-word meanings. Which part changes the meaning in each word? Read a connected sentence with each word.

Teacher: verify that pack and its sound–spelling pattern are known before selecting repack as an untaught transfer item. Replace it with an equally appropriate known base if needed and finalize before instruction. Do not teach the transfer word before assessing it.

Spelling: cover the printed list. Teacher dictates rebuild and helpful, one at a time in a neutral sentence; student writes them below.

5. Individual explanation and listening

After independent work is collected, explain one text-based reason to a partner. Paraphrase the partner’s idea accurately; then ask a relevant question or add a relevant contribution. Switch roles. Tell what you checked or reconsidered and why.

Teacher: preserve each learner’s actual words or a permitted recording reference. A partner product does not replace individual evidence. Record support, but do not supply the answer during the independent check.

Suggested connected sentences: “They rebuild the dam.” “A builder works on a structure.” “The helpful person gave assistance.” “I repack my bag.” Confirm the sentences and selected words suit the actual group. Metacognitive reflection supports analysis; it is not a substitute for demonstrated reading.
